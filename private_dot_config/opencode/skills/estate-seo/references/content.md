# Content — briefs, intent, on-page rules

## Keyword → intent clustering

Collect queries (Search Console, sales/support logs, competitor pages),
cluster by intent, keep the strongest cluster per page:

- informational ("what is X") → guide/definition page
- comparative ("X vs Y", "best X for Z") → comparison page with a table
- transactional ("X pricing", "buy X") → product/pricing page
- navigational → make sure the official page ranks, nothing more

One page per cluster; two pages competing for one intent cannibalize both.

## The brief (before writing)

Target query + intent; the one-sentence answer; section outline (H2/H3);
entities and subtopics the top-3 competitors cover that we must too;
internal links in/out (descriptive anchors); the page's unique angle —
what we have that they don't (original data, real experience, a method).

## On-page rules

- Title ≤60 chars: primary phrase + differentiator, no stuffing.
- Meta description ≤155 chars: the value + the action; unique per page.
- Answer in the first 100 words (featured-snippet and AI-citation shape).
- Descriptive subheadings a skimmer could navigate by.
- Internal links: descriptive anchors, from related pages, to and from.
- Update visible dates on real changes; keep a change log.

## E-E-A-T signals

Experience (first-hand detail: "measured on <date> with <method>"),
Expertise (correct depth, correct terminology), Authoritativeness (named
author, entity consistency, cited by others), Trust (accurate, dated,
correctable, secure site, real contact/policy pages).

## Copy quality floor

No filler ("simply", "powerful", "cutting-edge"); specific numbers with
units and dates; tables for comparisons; short paragraphs (2–4 sentences);
active voice; the reader's words, not the org chart's.
