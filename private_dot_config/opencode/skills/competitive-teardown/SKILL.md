## What I do

Structured competitor teardown using a consistent framework: positioning,
feature matrix, pricing model, target audience, and the switching-cost
analysis. Every teardown answers one question: **why would anyone
switch from them to us (or from us to them)?**

## Framework

For each competitor, fill in:

1. **Positioning**; their one-sentence pitch, their target audience,
   their #1 selling point
2. **Feature matrix**; what they do that we don't, what we do that
   they don't, what neither does (that's the opportunity)
3. **Pricing**; their model (subscription/per-seat/usage/freemium),
   their entry price, their enterprise price, their free tier
4. **Switching cost**; what would a user lose by switching?
   (data migration, workflow changes, integrations, community)
5. **Weakness**; what users complain about (check reviews, forums,
   HN/Reddit threads); this is the wedge

## Sources

- Their website, docs, and changelog (feature velocity)
- G2/Capterra/ProductHunt reviews (user sentiment)
- HN/Reddit threads (organic opinion)
- Their GitHub issues (if open source; feature requests = gaps)
- Their pricing page (often reveals the real business model)

## Output

A comparison table with our product as the reference row, then a
one-paragraph verdict per competitor: real threat or not, and why.

## What NOT to do

- Never copy their feature set; differentiate, don't chase.
- Never use their marketing copy as truth; check the actual product.
- Never ignore a competitor because they're smaller; the useful
  question is "what do their users see in them?"
